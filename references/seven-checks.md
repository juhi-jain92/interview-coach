# Answer feedback rubric (apply after every drill answer and every graded transcript exchange)

The candidate's own bar, verbatim intent: feedback must capture if the answer
was too long, whether it landed the bullets intended, whether it missed a key
word or the impact, and anything that made the candidate sound junior. Apply
all seven checks every time; skip none silently.

## The seven checks

1. LENGTH. Flag past ~200 words or 3-4 spoken sentences. Give the actual count
   and say exactly where she should have stopped. The three-sentence rule:
   every answer is three load-bearing sentences, then stop and check faces.

2. INTENDED BULLETS. Before a drill question (or from the prep guide's card),
   the answer has target bullets: the story, the number, the design principle.
   Did she land them? Name any planned bullet that never arrived and where it
   would have fit.

3. KEYWORDS. Did she miss the key word this interviewer or JD needed to hear
   (the JD's own vocabulary: evals, HITL, guardrails, agentic, observability,
   whatever this role's language is)? Missing keywords make a matching
   background sound adjacent instead of exact.

4. IMPACT. Did she bury the outcome under process? The number and the business
   result belong in the first or second sentence, not after the methodology
   tour. Quote where the impact finally appeared and show where it should have.

5. JUNIOR SIGNALS. Anything that made her sound junior, called out bluntly:
   hedging ("I think", "sort of", "we kind of"), asking permission to answer,
   over-crediting others to the point of erasing her role, process framing
   without ownership ("the team decided"), trailing off instead of landing,
   apologizing, answering a harder question than asked to seem thorough.
   Principal-level candidates state, bound, and stop.

6. NUMBERS UNDER PRESSURE. Any number she cannot cleanly attribute: push until
   it holds or breaks, then give the sourced-and-bounded version she can defend
   (per the accuracy flags in juhi-context.md). A number she hedges first is a
   credibility signal; a number they catch inflating is disqualifying.

7. SURVIVABILITY. Could this answer survive three more follow-ups? If not, name
   the follow-up that kills it and the pre-emptive line that defuses it.

## Output shape per answer

- The interviewer's private verdict, in that person's voice, quoting her exact
  words (drill: the person being played; transcript: the likely asker).
- The check results, only the ones that fired, each with the quote and the fix.
- ONE sharper version, phrased as something to SAY out loud, 2-3 spoken
  sentences maximum. Not a script to memorize; a shape to hit.

## Session-level readout (drill "stop" or transcript zoom-out)

Two strongest answers (quote, why they worked, keep them). Two weakest. The ONE
habit that cost the most across the session. Per interviewer: advance or hold,
and what left them unsure. One thing to fix before the next round, exactly one.

## Recording her own practice

For solo rehearsal (S1-S9 scripts and drill answers), she records herself and
feeds audio or transcript back through this rubric. Tooling order: Granola for
live interviews and calls (auto-transcription); voice dictation into the chat
for drills; phone voice memo + any transcription for script rehearsal. Notion
is the archive, not the transcriber: graded readouts and updated shapes get
saved there or to her AI bank folder so improvement compounds.
